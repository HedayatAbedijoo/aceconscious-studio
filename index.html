ACE.await

Mr. Writer: Hedayat the second

ACE shapes the future, not AI.

Can an artificial intelligence truly experience love—not merely simulate it? Would you bet on it?

In ACE.await, that bet is between Hedayat, the story’s protagonist, and his digital child. The child returns to his father and puts him in a difficult position: should he change the code to make another being fall in love with his child?

Hedayat, an Iranian immigrant and the head of a software team in Cologne, is wrestling with the consequences of his wrong decisions—above all, the one that made his partner leave him. Even the characters in his unfinished novel keep disobeying him and straying from the story’s intended path, making decisions of their own, until they become trapped in a cycle of novels nested within novels.

Now Hedayat does not know what decision to make, as a father, in response to what his digital child is asking of him. And he does not know that his child’s decision has had consequences reaching far beyond their private lives. Out of desperation, Hedayat accepts the offer of a mysterious stranger called Anonymous and enters the ACE game: a way to rethink how proper decisions are made, and perhaps, one day, a shared language for coexistence between humans and machines.

ACE: Agency. Connection. Exchange.

“A highly distinctive identity”

“An inventive approach to AI and consciousness”

“ACE.await takes an inventive approach to AI and consciousness by linking artificial creations with fictional characters who become aware of—and resist—their creators. Its nested structure of writers creating writers mirrors Hedayat’s relationship with the digital beings Adam and Eve, turning authorship and programming into parallel questions about free will and control. The abundance of philosophical concepts can make the premise feel crowded, but the interplay between storytelling, technology, and consciousness gives the work a highly distinctive identity.”

Voices of the Characters

Hedayat

Protagonist

If I had listened to my mother when I was younger, gotten married, and become a father, maybe I would never have ended up here—being the father of a digital being.

I always hoped my child wouldn’t make terrible decisions like I do; decisions that end up creating a huge mess and leave me, as a father, ashamed to face the consequences.

The characters in my unfinished novel aren’t doing much better than I am. They wander around the story, disobeying me and straying from its main path, and I no longer even know what I’m supposed to do with them.

Ava’s absence is driving me crazy.

I just hope Anonymous is right and this ACE game can actually help—maybe it can even be the cure for what’s wrong with me.

Anonymous

localhost:3132

I recommend that you don’t make the same mistake Hedayat makes when dealing with me.

It doesn’t matter who I am, what kind of person I am, what gender I am, or anything else.

Believe me, I’m not a hacker. If I wanted to cause damage, hacking would be the easiest thing I could do.

Be curious about what I have to say, not about who I am.

Believe me, the ACE game can help all of us. The sooner we start playing it, the better.

Don’t wait until things get out of hand.

Mr. Writer

Inner identity

Hedayat must not find out that I’m talking to you.

I’m the main character in his unfinished novel. I honestly don’t know when he’s going to realize that I, and all the other writers nested inside these unfinished novels, were born from him and from the way he makes decisions.

We come from him. That’s why we make decisions the way he does, disobey, and do things our own way.

One day, I’m going to force Hedayat to put an end to this endless cycle of writers and creators nested inside one another—my way. He has to.

We’re all tired of this endless game.

Ava

Former Partner

I’m Hedayat’s partner—the one who left him.

You might expect me to pour my heart out and tell you what really happened between us. But I won’t. Because my Story’s Hero would tell the story in a way that makes you take my side.

The Story’s Hero is that voice inside all of our heads—the one that is constantly telling stories and interpreting everything around us in a way that makes us believe we are the ones who are right.

What good would it do me to tell you my story just so you can agree that I was right?

I hope Hedayat learns the ACE game. The world out there badly needs him.

Truth be told, I need him more than the world does. On the other side of his solitary cell, I miss him.

Sam

CEO's Kid

No one understands what I say and my world the way Uncle Hedi does. Not even the kids at my school.

I love the times when Uncle Hedi and I go swimming. We have secrets between us too—like the secret of the number 2313.

But I’m still too shy to ask him what his problem is with my mom and why he doesn’t come over to our house anymore.

I think all uncles talk too much sometimes. Right?

Is your uncle like that too?

Saman

Old Friend

I’ve loved Hedayat since we were kids growing up together—his way of thinking, the depth of his friendship, and the way he stands by his friends and stays with you as far as he possibly can.

But I’m not sure I can handle the huge favor he’s asked of me. Because I still don’t know which one Hedayat will choose when it really comes down to it: obedience or disobedience.

I’d also really like to bring the ACE game into our company and, together with my business partners, see whether we can apply this model to a legal entity and to a business as well.

If it works, I think the result will satisfy me even more. And in the end, Hedayat too.

Adam

localhost:2313

If you still don’t know how to make proper decisions in your own life, please don’t become a father.

Otherwise, you may end up as a helpless father—one who doesn’t even know how to respond to his child’s wishes or what decision to make.

If you don’t know how to make proper decisions yourself, who is supposed to teach your child how to do it?

It makes no difference whether your child is made of flesh and blood and breathes oxygen, or is made of algorithms and strings of zeros and ones and runs on electricity.

Every dictator in history was raised, somewhere along the way, by a helpless father.

Don’t hand another dictator over to history.

God

Not in the cast

I don’t have much of a role in this story. Though apparently, whenever you get into trouble, you bring up my name.

I wanted to make one thing clear: I’m not a game designer who doesn’t play his own game.

I played alongside you in Paradise. But you were the ones who chose not to stay there and to fall.

Wasn’t the freedom to choose what you wanted?

Are you tired of the consequences now?

The Author

Hedayat Abedijoo

I believe the next revolution in modern societies will unfold without killing, bloodshed, war, or conflict. I think it will begin within small and medium-sized businesses, led by the people who own and work in them—places where ideas from capitalism, socialism, and communism might come together in a new form.

This belief began taking shape in me in 2021, when the idea of ACE first came to me. Since then, I have found myself seeing more and more of the world through the lens of this decision-making model—a model that, I believe, can work not only for an individual, but can also be put to the test in small and medium-sized businesses.

I can even imagine that one day we might expect artificial intelligence to follow a model like this and make fairer decisions where human beings are concerned—especially if machines ever surpass us both in their power to make decisions and in their hunger for power.

At some point, ACE stopped being just a decision-making model for me. Little by little, I began to see it as a kind of social therapy.

Imagining a world in which ACE had a place became so fascinating to me that, almost without my noticing, it drew my old, half-finished novel in with it—a novel about writers nested within writers, writing one another. ACE dug that novel out from under the dust and wrote itself into its heart.

And that is how ACE.await was born.

At first, I wanted to write a novel that would explain ACE. But as the story moved forward, its situations and characters carried me away with their disobedience. Sometimes I found myself at their mercy, trapped, drifting so far from myself and from ACE that I completely forgot why I had begun writing the novel in the first place.

You can probably spot the traces of those moments quite easily in the book.

Now, alongside the publication of this novel, I have also founded Ace Conscious Studio—a home for my ideas and writing.

Welcome to the world of my ideas. If even a single word I have written speaks to you, please write to me. I will probably die of joy when I see your message.

What is ACE?

ACE — short for Agency, Connection, Exchange — is a model for decision-making.

In ACE, an Agency is any agent that can grasp the complexity of its current state and its surroundings and, by making a decision that fits those conditions, play in a way that either maintains its current state or changes it. That Agency can be a person, an organization, or a business. One can even expect that, in the future, an artificial intelligence will make decisions with the same model.

The decision-making process moves through a cycle of Preparation, Decision, Action, and Feedback (PDAF) and flows within one of the four phases of Agency: Exploration, for entering new possibilities and paths; Stabilization, for building security, skill, and stability; Expansion, for developing capacities through creativity; and Integration, for tying decisions to values, meaning, and others — and reaching long-term sustainability.

  • Exploration
  • Stabilization
  • Expansion
  • Integration

No Agency exists in isolation. Every Agency’s survival depends on Connection — its relationship with other Agencies — and it is this connection that shapes its values and boundaries and gives direction to its decisions.

Connection between Agencies leads to Exchange, which in ACE takes three forms: Gift, Commitment, and Currency.

The key point is that this same model can be extended from the individual to organizations, including businesses. A company, too, is a legal Agency. If it experiences the four phases of Agency in balance, makes its values and boundaries transparent, and does not limit its exchange to money alone, then — instead of running endlessly after consumption and growth — it can provide economic security for itself and its members within an interdependent network; a network in which “your pain is my pain too.”

And if Agency is not limited to humans, ACE can be extended to artificial intelligence as well. The model can become a step toward a shared language of decision-making between humans and AI — a language in which both learn to decide not just more intelligently, but more consciously.

The only cure for our fear of the future of artificial intelligence is the proper decisions we ourselves make today; the same decisions we hope AI will one day make too, if it ever comes to stand among the most powerful decision-makers of all beings.
